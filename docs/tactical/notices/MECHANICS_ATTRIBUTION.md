The independent noncommercial study uses a predeclared adult-only paired cohort, machine-pitch context and foot-plant body angles to examine exit-velocity associations. Transformations include unique joins, fixed cohort selection, explicit target/alias exclusions and athlete-grouped validation. It does not estimate health, injury risk, professional-game ability or causal coaching effects. Source sensor measurements are named separately. Public artifacts are aggregate-only: no participant identifiers, pseudonymous rankings, individual body profiles, trial rows, fold predictions or trained weights.

## BYU serve-execution study

Credit Nate Sandholtz and the authors/contributors of [byu_tennis_serves](https://github.com/nsandholtz/byu_tennis_serves), revision `fd435d9158ad5671c819bee3c47a47d7161a42cb`. The original MIT copyright/license notice is preserved in `BYU_MIT.txt`.

The project models recorded non-let attempts from eight NCAA men, using stated targets and observed net/bounce measurements. It explicitly excludes 19 lets from the 567-attempt source and does not invent bounce positions for net-censored attempts. Leave-player-out comparisons do not establish future-session or professional transfer. Participant ages are unverified; reports remain aggregate-only without participant or trial identifiers, individual profiles or small-cell disclosures.

The primary paper reports BYU IRB #2025-487 determined that the study fell outside its regulatory definition of human-subjects research. This is not represented as IRB approval or evidence about individual ages or consent. No source author or provider endorsement is implied.
